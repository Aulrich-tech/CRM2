Module 3: CRM and Sales Strategy

Lesson 1 covers sales. Lesson 2 covers marketing.

Lesson 1: CRM and Sales Strategy

Sales Strategy

Sales StrategyInvolves planning how an organization identifies potential customers.
CRMSupports these activities by organizing customer information and providing tools.
  • Allows the sales team to move from relying mainly on personal records and manual follow-ups to a more organized, data-driven sales process.
  • By helping organizations understand customers and manage sales activities more systematically.

Sales Representatives Use CRM Information to Determine

  • Which customers are potential buyers
  • Which leads require follow-up
  • Which opportunities are close to a purchase
  • Which customers have not been contacted recently
  • Which products or services a customer previously considered
  • Which sales activities are producing results

CRM in Sales Strategy

  1. Customer Information
  2. Lead
  3. Opportunity
  4. Sales Action
  5. Purchase
  6. Follow-up

Customer Data

  • Information that can support their decisions.
  • Used by sales employees to understand the customer's situation before contacting them.

Incomplete or outdated information can lead to inappropriate sales actions.

Sales Representatives May Review Before Contacting Customers

Customer InformationPossible Sales Use
Previous purchasesIdentify related products or services
Purchase frequencyIdentify regular customers
Previous inquiriesDetermine areas of interest
Communication historyAvoid repeating previous discussions
Customer preferencesAdjust the sales approach
Previous sales opportunitiesDetermine whether follow-up is needed

Lead Management

LeadA potential customer who may have an interest in an organization's product or service.

CRM helps record, organize, and monitor leads.

Lead Management Includes

  • Recording new leads
  • Capturing contact information
  • Identifying the source of the lead
  • Assigning leads to sales employees
  • Recording communication
  • Scheduling follow-ups
  • Monitoring lead status

Opportunity Management

OpportunityRepresents a potential business transaction that has progressed beyond an initial lead.

Different Stages of the Sales Process

  1. Lead
  2. Qualified Lead
  3. Opportunity
  4. Proposal/Quotation
  5. Negotiation
  6. Closed Sale

This identifies which opportunities require attention.

Sales Force Automation (SFA)

  • CRM technology to automate and organize routine sales activities.
  • Reduces the need for manual management of sales tasks.

Common SFA Functions

  • Lead management
  • Contact management
  • Opportunity management
  • Sales activity tracking
  • Follow-up reminders
  • Quotation and proposal management
  • Sales pipeline monitoring
  • Sales forecasting
  • Sales performance reporting

Customer Follow-Up and Retention

  • Helps maintain the relationship and identify future sales opportunities.
  • Does not end on customer purchase.

CRM Can Help Track

  • Scheduled follow-ups
  • Previous customer concerns
  • Product or service inquiries
  • Purchase history
  • Pending requests
  • Future sales opportunities

CRM-Supported Follow-Up

  1. Purchase
  2. Follow-up
  3. Customer Support
  4. Additional Need
  5. Future Sales Opportunity

Sales Performance

  • Information that can be used to evaluate sales performance.
CRM MetricPerformance indicators that track how well a business attracts customers (the slide ends at "attracts").

CRM to Improve Sales Strategy

  1. CRM Data
  2. Sales Analysis
  3. Identify Problem
  4. Adjust Strategy
  5. Implement Action
  6. Measure Results

A Company May Discover That

  • Certain leads have higher conversion rates
  • Some products generate more purchases
  • Customers respond better to specific follow-ups
  • Some stages of the sales process cause delay
  • Certain sales activities produce better results than others

Lesson 2: CRM and Marketing Strategy

Marketing Strategy

Marketing StrategyProviding information about customers and their interactions with the organization.
CRMSupports marketing activity by organizing customer information and providing tools.
  • Allows organizations to move beyond broad, one-size-fits-all marketing.

A CRM System Can Help Marketers Understand

  • Who the customers are
  • What products or services they purchase
  • How frequently they purchase
  • What customers respond to in campaigns
  • Which customers are highly engaged
  • Which customers may need to be re-engaged

Marketing Process

  1. Customer Data
  2. Customer Analysis
  3. Marketing Strategy
  4. Campaign
  5. Customer Response
  6. Performance Measurement

Customer Data for Marketing Decisions

Customer DataProvides the foundation for CRM-supported marketing decisions.
Customer DataMarketing Use
Customer profileIdentify target customers
Purchase historyIdentify product interests
Purchase frequencyIdentify regular customers
Customer preferencesDevelop relevant offers
Campaign responsesEvaluate customer interest
Website or digital interactionsIdentify engagement
Previous communicationsPersonalize future communication

Importance of Data Quality

Marketing decisions are only as useful as the information on which they are based. Incomplete, outdated, or inaccurate customer data can result in:

  • Incorrect customer targeting
  • Irrelevant promotions
  • Duplicate communication
  • Poor customer experiences
  • Misleading campaign results

Customer Segmentation

Customer SegmentationProcess of dividing customers into groups based on shared characteristics, behaviors, or needs.

CRM helps organizations create customer segments using available customer data.

Customers May Be Grouped According To

  • Demographic characteristics
  • Purchasing behavior
  • Purchase frequency
  • Customer value
  • Product preferences
  • Level of engagement
  • Previous campaign responses

An Online Retailer May Divide Its Customers Into

Customer SegmentPossible Marketing Strategy
New customersWelcome offers
Frequent customersLoyalty reward
High-value customersExclusive offers
Inactive customersRe-engagement campaigns
Customers interested in a productProduct-specific promotions

Targeted and Personalized Marketing

Segmented organizations can create marketing activities designed for particular groups.

Targeted MarketingFocuses marketing efforts on a specific customer segment.
Personalized MarketingAdapting marketing communication or offers to an individual customer's information or behavior.

Examples

  • Using a customer's name in an email
  • Recommending products based on purchase history
  • Offering products related to previous purchases
  • Sending reminders based on customer activity
  • Providing customized promotions

Marketing Automation

Marketing AutomationUses technology to automate selected marketing activities and processes.

CRM systems can support marketing automation by allowing organizations to schedule and manage activities such as:

  • Email campaigns
  • Customer follow-ups
  • Promotional messages
  • Customer segmentation
  • Re-engagement campaigns
  • Campaign notifications
  • Campaign reporting

Automated Sequence for a New Customer

  1. Customer Registration
  2. Welcome Email
  3. Product Information
  4. Promotional Offer
  5. Follow-Up Message

Benefits of Marketing Automation

  • Reduce repetitive manual work
  • Improve consistency of communication
  • Reach customers at appropriate stages
  • Manage larger numbers of customers
  • Monitor campaign activities
  • Respond to customer behavior

Marketing Campaigns

Marketing campaigns may involve several activities:

  1. Identify the target customer segment
  2. Define the campaign objective
  3. Develop the marketing message
  4. Select the communication channel
  5. Launch the campaign
  6. Monitor customer responses
  7. Analyze campaign results
  8. Improve future campaigns

Organizations Can Then Monitor

  • How many customers received the campaign
  • How many opened the message
  • How many responded
  • How many made a purchase
  • Which offers generated the strongest response

Measuring Marketing Performance

Allows organizations to evaluate marketing activities using customer and campaign data.

Marketing MetricWhat It Measures
Campaign reachNumber of customers reached
Engagement rateCustomer interaction with the campaign
Response rateCustomers who responded
Conversion rateCustomers who completed the desired action
Repeat purchase rateCustomers who purchased again
Customer retentionAbility to maintain customers
Campaign revenueRevenue generated by the campaign

These metrics determine whether a campaign is producing meaningful results.

Using CRM to Improve Marketing Strategy

Allows marketers to compare campaign results and customer responses. The process can be summarized as:

  1. Customer Data
  2. Campaign Planning
  3. Campaign Execution
  4. Customer Response
  5. Performance Analysis
  6. Strategy Improvement

The Marketing Team May Use These Findings To

  • Increase personalized recommendations
  • Improve customer segmentation
  • Develop more relevant offers
  • Adjust future campaigns
  • Monitor whether the improved strategy produces better results
